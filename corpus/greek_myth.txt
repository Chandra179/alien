Icarus flew too close to the sun, and the wax that bound his wings melted, sending him plummeting into the sea.
Pandora opened the jar she was forbidden to touch, releasing every sorrow and pestilence upon mankind, leaving only hope inside.
Achilles was dipped in the River Styx as an infant, held by the heel, making him invulnerable everywhere except that single point.
Sisyphus was condemned to roll a boulder to the top of a hill for eternity, only to watch it roll back down each time he neared the summit.
Narcissus fell in love with his own reflection in a pool of water, unable to look away until he withered and died beside it.
Prometheus stole fire from the gods and gave it to humanity, and for his theft he was chained to a rock where an eagle ate his liver each day.
The labyrinth of Crete held the Minotaur, a creature half-man and half-bull, until Theseus followed a thread to find his way back out.
Orpheus descended into the underworld to retrieve his beloved Eurydice, but he looked back too soon and lost her forever.
Persephone was gathering flowers when the earth split open and Hades carried her down to be queen of the dead for part of every year.
Arachne boasted she could weave better than Athena; the goddess turned her into a spider, doomed to spin webs for all eternity.
Midas asked that everything he touched turn to gold, and soon he could not eat, drink, or touch his own daughter without transforming them.
Daedalus crafted wings of feathers and wax for himself and his son, warning the boy not to soar too high nor fly too low.
